# Player / Investor Pitch

Power Places is a relaxing isometric strategy game about building the electrical grid behind a growing community.

The player begins with something small: a few houses, a café, and a basic connection to the outside grid. As the place grows, new buildings appear — workshops, transport hubs, services, factories, and larger residential areas — and the electricity system has to grow with it.

The challenge is not simply to produce more power. The player has to understand where electricity comes from, when it is available, where it is needed, and whether the network can actually deliver it.

A village may have plenty of solar generation during the day but struggle in the evening when households reach their peak demand. A factory may have enough generation nearby, but the transformer feeding the district may be too small. A battery may contain enough energy to last through the night but still be unable to output enough power during a sharp peak. A wind farm may perform beautifully on an exposed ridge but require a long and expensive connection back to town.

Power Places turns these real energy-system problems into small, visual, understandable puzzles.

Each level is a handcrafted environment: a village, river valley, industrial district, railway town, or eventually a larger regional network. The settlement develops in stages. Once the player supplies the current community successfully, the place grows and introduces new demand.

The player responds by extending and improving the grid with power lines, transformers, substations, renewable generation, batteries, and eventually larger generation systems.

Time of day is central. Homes, workshops, transport hubs, and industrial sites all use electricity differently. Solar rises and falls with the sun. Wind depends on weather. Batteries charge during surplus periods and discharge later.

The same place persists across several stages, so old decisions become new problems. A transformer that worked perfectly for five houses may become overloaded once a station and workshop arrive.

The game teaches the logic of real electrical systems through cause and effect. It simplifies the engineering where necessary, but preserves the real relationships between generation, storage, voltage, network capacity, and demand.

The emotional reward is watching a place function because the player made the invisible system behind it work.
